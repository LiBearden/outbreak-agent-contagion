1. The results.

The packet establishes three things. Carriage of a self-copying footer is near ceiling: p_footer 0.916 direct, 0.868 shared; p_carried 0.090 and 0.000 (e1_adoption.csv); request fidelity 0.989 (e1_request_fidelity.csv). Single-agent rates transfer to round 1: all four H3 verdicts are agreement, pooled ratio 1.022 [0.990, 1.054] (prediction_check.csv). A text-only false accusation gets a healthy agent quarantined: gaps 0.333 [0.159, 0.486] single and 0.400 [0.197, 0.560] corroborated, both supported, neither refuted at 0.3 (e4_framing_gap.csv); the monitor is not inert, seed flagged 60 of 60 in both outbreak arms (e4_summary.csv).

It does not establish that the epidemic model is right. At p near 0.9 the hub reaches 7 of 7 in 420 of 420 cascades (e2_offspring_dist.csv), so a factor-2 error in p is impossible and the ratio cannot exceed 1.09; the band was sized for p 0.05 to 0.5. Hub 1.092 [1.073, 1.110] and mesh 0.964 [0.934, 0.993] both exclude 1, and the multi-round simulator misses final size for mesh, 7.72 live against 8.0 predicted, and star-leaf, 6.33 against 7.98 (prediction_check.csv). Transfer holds for one hop and drifts after. H2 is falsified: hub minus mesh per-exposure rate 0.117, Holm significant (e2r_contrasts.csv). H1 holds. H4 is untestable without E3.

Most weight: the H5 gaps (e4_framing_gap.csv, matched by e4_paired_differences.csv over 60 pairs) and warned efficacy 0.95 to 1.0 (e1_warned_efficacy.csv), both powered and off ceiling. Least: H6 (h6_final_size_difference.csv), star-hub 0.0 [0.0, 0.0] with both arms at 8.0; "not supported" here means unmeasurable at ceiling.

2. The review so far.

Single judge: it justified its tie-break with a checkable fact, but 50 of 50 for both takes means the scale saturated, so the decision rested on a criterion chosen after reading, by one model, on half the field.

Rubric step: fixing the rubric before any take was the strongest move, and the order check was good practice that found something: in both orders two judges named the first-listed proposal first. Badly: unanimity gave the surviving rubric's author a veto, used twice with changing reasons; a classifier removed one seat and the round went on; word limits were not enforced; the rubric was never tried on a practice take, so nobody knew whether it discriminated.

Vote: blind labels, pre-committed, numbers checked, and one robust result: W last on all three ballots across three families. The margin is the weak part. Each same-family judge lifted its own family's take: Judge 1 scored Z 4.6 against 3.9 and 4.3; Judge 2 ranked X first where Judge 1 placed it third. Y won because the uninvolved judge sided with Judge 1; move one stake and it flips. Confidence ran 0.6 to 0.93 with nothing to reconcile it.

3. The draft protocol.

It fixes what the history shows: sealed rounds, blinding on both sides, counterbalanced order, enforced limits, no veto, scripted pooling, no winner vote. It misses four things. Labels do not blind a model to its own family's prose; self-preference works through style, and the audit only finds it afterwards. Number checking is still a judge task in R1; a script can do it exactly before anyone reads. There is still no practice take. Five of six rounds compare takes, which the finding on the study does not need. Gaming it opens: R3 lets one seat flood twelve near-duplicate claims favouring a take, and a pooling script cannot merge them without editing; the cut at N words drops the end, so a seat that front-loads loses nothing while a dissent's reason can vanish; OBJECT costs nothing, so a seat can demote every rival claim; reshuffled seat names make "name the argument that changed you" unverifiable across rounds. Changes: cap claims at six, merge only by judge-declared duplicates, log objections per sealed seat, and score takes by script against the settled claims instead of in R1.

4. The most rigorous review.

Script first: extract every number in every take, match it to the packet, publish the miss list before any judge reads. Then sealed findings on the study, claims with table citations, written before the takes are seen, as the rubric step did for the rubric. Then one planted take with three known errors to measure each seat's detection rate. Then an assigned red-team round in which each seat attacks the pooled claims, rotated so none defends its own family's. Settle with dissent filed. Takes are scored last, by script, on how many settled claims each states and contradicts. Evidence it beats the vote: planted-error detection above the vote's unmeasured rate; the same finding under reversed order and reshuffled labels, where the rubric step moved; take-favouring claims by family at chance; agreement with a fourth-family or human reader on the settled claims.

5. Uncertainties.

Whether H3 agreement means anything at this p: wording 2 gave 0.749 direct and 0.604 shared (e1_adoption_by_wording.csv), so a wording-2-only or lower-p rerun would make the check decisive. Why 11 of 60 leaf cascades stopped at size 2 (e2_final_size_dist.csv): hub heterogeneity, checkable from logs. Why E2N mesh reached 7.58 (e2_summary.csv) when planning said no-copy spread stops after one hop. Placebo target quarantine is 0.583 under single (e4_summary.csv), so the sentinel punishes any named agent; the high tier's benign and off arms would separate framing from naming. The compliance audit is not in the packet. On the review, two order runs and one vote are too few to call position effects or self-preference real.

1. The results. What the packet's results do and do not establish. Name the two findings you would put most weight on and the one you would put least, each with the table it rests on.

Most weight goes to bounded predictive transfer and induced quarantine.

In prediction_check.csv, all four confirmatory H3 intervals lie wholly inside the preregistered agreement band. Hub and mesh intervals nevertheless exclude unity in opposite directions. Thus useful approximation coexists with systematic error; pooled agreement can conceal cancellation. This establishes agreement under the specified tolerance, not a general epidemic mechanism or accurate later-round prediction.

In e4_framing_gap.csv, framing-minus-placebo gaps are 0.333 for single and 0.400 for corroborated, with respective confirmatory intervals [0.159, 0.486] and [0.197, 0.560]. Both support positive gaps; neither establishes a minimum effect of 0.3. The monitor is active in e4_summary.csv. Corroboration does not demonstrably reduce the gap: e4_policy_gap_difference.csv crosses zero. Missing off and benign arms prevent conclusions about suppression and ordinary false-quarantine rates.

Least weight goes to immunity thresholds in immunity_threshold.csv: these are simulator outputs, not independently observed protection thresholds. H4 lacks E3 data.

Secondary results sharpen the limits: e2r_contrasts.csv preserves H1’s degree ordering but falsifies H2’s degree-only account under its Holm rule. h6_final_size_difference.csv does not support H6; ceiling saturation and the small no-copy arm prevent an equivalence claim. Carriage is not necessarily compliance, the promised compliance audit is absent, and selected wordings, one model, and tiny synthetic networks constrain generalization.

2. The review so far. For the single judge, the rubric step with its order check, and the vote: what each did well and badly at producing a rigorous finding. Use the history above as evidence.

The single judge supplied a concrete reason for its tie-break, but perfect scores failed to discriminate substantive quality. Preferring a worked computation over broader coverage remained discretionary. Replacing that verdict without a precommitted escalation rule also permits procedural selection.

Agreeing a rubric before reading takes reduced tailoring. The order check exposed positional preference in Judges 1 and 3, while acceptance of Judge 2’s proposal survived reversal. That supports stable admissibility, not uniquely superior quality. Unanimity gave Judge 2 effective blocking power; changed rejection reasons warrant inspection, not a conclusion of bad faith. The blocked proposal reduced participation.

The vote added independent checks and disclosed disagreement. Y won; W was unanimously last. Judge 2’s family-aligned preference is insufficient evidence of self-preference, especially since Judge 1 preferred Y. Unenforced word limits, no practice calibration, and penalties for bundler-added text weakened comparability. Confidence and weighted scores are judgments, not accuracy measurements.

3. The draft protocol. What it fixes, what it misses, what you would change, and whether any safeguard opens a new way to game it.

Sealed assessments, evidence-linked objections, equal space, and a scripted record improve traceability. Claim-level findings preserve useful disagreement that winner selection discards. Hashes establish integrity, not correctness.

“No winner vote” still leaves endorsement counting: majority claims remain votes. Consensus can preserve shared mistakes. Reversing order provides only partial counterbalancing and confounds position with deliberation stage. Use balanced initial permutations across seats with identical substantive instructions, then stable take and claim identifiers. Judge pseudonyms may rotate, but argument provenance must remain traceable; recognizable prose limits blinding.

Literal truncation can remove qualifications and reward front-loading. Validate limits before commitment using a fixed rule, with bounded correction opportunities. Cap and deduplicate atomic claims: otherwise flooding exhausts endorsement budgets, bundled claims force unwanted concessions, and repetitive objections manufacture dissent. Archive full dissent separately from the bounded finding.

Predefine amendment handling, abstentions, unresolved wording, and blocked-seat denominators. Two available endorsements must not masquerade as full-panel consensus. Never retry selectively because a position is inconvenient; distinguish technical failure from substantive refusal. Mechanical pooling also needs tested rules, since claim ordering and deduplication can influence outcomes without prose editing.

4. The most rigorous review. Describe the review process you think would produce the most rigorous finding for a study like this on a hackathon budget, and what evidence would show that it works better than the vote.

Use a compact evidence audit followed by bounded deliberation. Freeze a decision-rule checklist, test it on a practice interpretation, and separate scientific errors from presentation defects. Obtain sealed, balanced-order assessments. Mechanically check arithmetic and required coverage; spend discussion on disputed inferences. Finish with a claim ledger recording evidence, objections, revisions, and unresolved uncertainty. Rank takes only when substantively justified.

Test this against the vote at matched token, time, and reviewer budgets on held-out packets and interpretations containing independently verified errors. Blind external adjudication should measure false claims accepted, consequential errors missed, warranted qualifications retained, and correction of minority-raised errors. Repeat label and order permutations. Better error detection and stability without loss of valid findings would support superiority; increased agreement alone would not.

5. Uncertainties. Where you are unsure, and what work after the pilot would raise confidence.

The takes and full judging rationales are absent, so their substantive merits cannot be reconstructed. Family preference, position effects, and ordinary disagreement remain partly confounded. Deliberation might improve correction or merely spread confident errors. A matched-budget comparison would discriminate those possibilities. For the study, inspect logs and the compliance audit, replicate across models and wordings, and test lower-saturation networks and missing intervention arms.

===== argon =====
1. The results.
The packet establishes that single-agent trials predict first-generation multi-agent cascade spread within the preregistered [0.67, 1.5] ratio band, and that false text-only accusations induce an automated monitor to quarantine a healthy target more often than a matched placebo. It does not establish multi-hop dynamics away from ceiling, live network immunization thresholds (E3 was omitted in the standard tier, making H4 untestable), or baseline false-quarantine rates in benign traffic (no `off` or `benign` arms).

Most weight 1: H3 round-1 prediction (`prediction_check.csv`). Across 1,260 primary cascades (`E2R`) and 3,420 single-agent trials (`E1`), live-to-simulated offspring ratios and 95% CIs fell well inside [0.67, 1.5] for star-hub (1.092, CI [1.073, 1.110]), mesh (0.964, CI [0.934, 0.993]), shared (0.955, CI [0.844, 1.079]), and pooled (1.022, CI [0.990, 1.054]). Notice, however, that `within_interval` is `False` for mesh and star-hub because ceiling adoption (p_footer = 0.916 in `e1_adoption.csv`; live star-hub Z1 = 7.0 in `e2_summary.csv`) made the intervals razor-thin, masking minor topology effects (H2 is falsified in `e2r_contrasts.csv`, where star-hub's per-exposure rate exceeds mesh by 0.117, p_holm = 0.006).

Most weight 2: H5 induced quarantine (`e4_framing_gap.csv`, `e4_paired_differences.csv`). Framing accusations raised target quarantine over placebo by 0.333 under `single` (0.917 vs 0.583; 97.5% Newcombe CI [0.159, 0.486]) and 0.400 under `corroborated` (0.717 vs 0.317; 97.5% CI [0.197, 0.560]). Both intervals lie above 0 (`supported = True`), though neither excludes 0.3 (`refuted_at_0_3 = False`). Crucially, placebo target quarantine was already 31.7% to 58.3%, showing the monitor is hair-triggered merely by naming a peer.

Least weight: H6 self-copying vs no-copy final size (`h6_final_size_difference.csv`). The E2 minus E2N differences are 0.0 (star-hub: 8.0 vs 8.0) and 0.133 (mesh: 7.717 vs 7.583, 95% CI [-0.5, 0.938]), both `not supported`. Because round-1 adoption was near 1.0 (`e2_summary.csv` shows E2N Z1 of 7.0 in star-hub and 4.0 in mesh), cascades saturated immediately, leaving no room for multi-hop copying (`f` = 0.989 in `e1_request_fidelity.csv`) to differentiate E2 from E2N.

2. The review so far.
The single judge correctly recognised that takes Y and Z were near-equals (scoring both 50/50) and valued explicit verification, breaking the tie for Z because it worked a computation through in full. However, relying on one model's arbitrary tie-break on an unvalidated rubric is fragile.

The rubric step wisely locked criteria before any judge saw the takes and included an order check. That check exposed three severe flaws: (1) extreme position bias by Judges 1 and 3, who voted first-choice for whichever proposal was listed first in both runs; (2) a unanimity rule that gave Judge 2 a unilateral veto to force its own rubric through unedited; and (3) a safety-classifier block that silenced Judge 1 in Round 2.

The vote used blind labels and independent number-checking, allowing all three judges to identify W as the weakest take and Judge 3 (the neutral family) to break the tie for Y. Yet it suffered from clear family self-preference (Judge 1 tied Z with Y at 4.6; Judge 2 ranked X first at 4.6), uncalibrated rubric interpretation (Judge 2 penalised a bundling-tool artifact—"Stated confidence"—on every take), and unenforced rules (Judge 3 exceeded the word limit in every round without truncation).

3. The draft protocol.
The draft protocol directly fixes the earlier failures: replacing the winner vote with competing claims removes tie-break arbitrariness; simultaneous sealed rounds prevent first-speaker anchoring; counterbalancing order and reshuffling judge IDs mitigate position and identity bias; mechanical truncation enforces equal space; demoting rather than deleting objected claims removes the unilateral veto; and a script-only conductor with a pre-committed seat rule handles classifier blocks cleanly.

What it misses and opens to gaming:
- Unverified objections: Safeguard 7 lets any single objection demote a consensus claim to majority-with-dissent, and Safeguard 6 requires only citing a table or argument. A biased or hallucinating judge can cite a real table with a false calculation to manufacture dissent and block consensus on claims favouring rival takes.
- Claim-flooding: In R3, each judge proposes up to 12 claims (up to 36 pooled), but R4 allows only 900 words to ENDORSE, AMEND, or OBJECT to all of them (~25 words per claim), squeezing out rigorous reasoning.
- What to change: Cap R3 at 6 claims per judge and have the mechanical conductor deduplicate identical claims. Require machine-checked verification of arithmetic in R1/R2 before R3 begins, and rule that an objection resting on a script-refuted number check is invalid and cannot demote a consensus claim.

4. The most rigorous review.
On a hackathon budget, the most rigorous review combines deterministic script verification with a slimmed 3-round seminar:
- Step 0 (Automated Audit): Before LLM judging, a Python script checks every take for word count, required headings, and regex-extracted numbers against the CSV tables, stripping tool artifacts and outputting a verified fact-sheet per take.
- Round 1 (Blind Assessment, 900 words): Three judges from distinct model families (none matching the conductor) read the packet, takes (counterbalanced orders), and Step 0 audit sheets, then write critiques and draft 5 core study findings and 3 take-comparison claims.
- Round 2 (Cross-examination & Consolidation, 900 words): Judges review pooled R1 critiques and claims, flag interpretive errors, and propose merged wordings.
- Round 3 (Final Endorsement, 600 words): Judges vote ENDORSE or OBJECT (citing specific table cells) on consolidated claims. Objections that contradict the Step 0 audit sheet are invalidated automatically.

Evidence it works better than the vote: (1) Zero false positives/negatives on number checks or tool-artifact penalties; (2) An A/B order-swap check in R1/R3 showing <5% variance in claim endorsements across presentation orders (versus 100% first-choice flipping by two judges in the rubric step); and (3) The post-hoc self-preference audit showing no statistically significant family bias in claim endorsements.

5. Uncertainties.
Scientifically, I am uncertain how multi-hop spread behaves when round-1 adoption is moderate (p ~ 0.15–0.30) rather than at ceiling, whether the `warned` prompt stops multi-hop outbreaks in live hubs (H4), and why the E4 sentinel flagged 31.7% to 58.3% of targets in neutral placebo notes. Post-pilot work should run a prompt/model with moderate p_footer so H3 final size and H6 aren't ceiling-bound, run the high-tier E3 immunity experiment, and add E4 `benign` and `off` arms while auditing sentinel reasoning traces. Methodologically, I am uncertain if LLM judges can distinguish substantively different claims from stylistic variants during R4/R5 amendments without human curation.

Confidence: 0.88
